# Speak to AI

**Speak to AI** is a practical prompt framework for clearer instructions, safer AI output, and repeatable human-directed workflows.

At the center of the project is the **11S Method**, a structured way to turn vague AI requests into controlled, testable prompts. The goal is not to make prompts longer. The goal is to make prompts work.

Speak to AI was shaped from real constraint: after a paralyzing stroke, AI became more than a productivity tool. It became a communication bridge, a planning assistant, a memory aid, a drafting partner, and a way to connect technology to daily independence.

That origin matters. This is not magic-language prompting. It is instruction design for people who need AI to be useful, accurate, and accountable.

---

## What Speak to AI Does

Speak to AI helps users:

- give AI clearer instructions,
- reduce hallucinations and unsupported claims,
- define source material and boundaries,
- organize complex requests,
- improve drafts without losing meaning,
- create reusable prompts,
- audit weak assumptions,
- build safer legal, business, coding, education, and operations workflows,
- and require a final quality check before relying on output.

The basic premise is simple:

> Better instructions produce better reasoned output.

## The 11S Method

| # | Element | What It Controls |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Structure** | The organization, order, and layout of the response |
| 2 | **Schema** | The required fields, sections, variables, tables, or reusable format |
| 3 | **Syntax** | The exact wording, command form, sentence rules, and formatting requirements |
| 4 | **Semantics** | The intended meaning of key terms and instructions |
| 5 | **Specificity** | The concrete facts, details, examples, constraints, and exclusions |
| 6 | **Scope** | What the AI should include, avoid, stop at, or reserve for later |
| 7 | **Source** | The materials, authorities, facts, files, law, data, or evidence the AI may rely on |
| 8 | **Safeguards** | Rules that prevent hallucination, overreach, unsafe output, or unsupported conclusions |
| 9 | **Style** | Tone, audience, reading level, professional voice, and rhetorical limits |
| 10 | **Sequence** | The order of reasoning, drafting, review, revision, and output |
| 11 | **Stress Test** | The final check for errors, gaps, contradictions, assumptions, and weak support |

See [`docs/11S_METHOD.md`](docs/11S_METHOD.md) for the full plain-English breakdown.

## Quick Start Prompt Formula

Use this simple structure when writing a prompt:

```text
Role:
You are a [specific expert role].

Task:
Create [specific deliverable].

Context:
Use the following facts, materials, and background: [insert context].

Rules:
Follow these constraints and avoid these errors: [insert rules].

Source:
Rely only on: [insert sources].

Format:
Return the answer in this structure: [insert format].

Quality Check:
Before finalizing, check for missing facts, unsupported claims, unclear wording, and weak assumptions.
```

## Core Rule

A good AI prompt should tell the system:

1. What role to play.
2. What task to perform.
3. What facts to use.
4. What sources to rely on.
5. What rules to follow.
6. What format to return.
7. What mistakes to avoid.
8. How to check the final answer.

That is the heart of Speak to AI.

---

## Mini 11S Checklist

Before submitting a prompt, ask:

- Did I define the result I want?
- Did I give the AI enough context?
- Did I specify the format?
- Did I identify the audience?
- Did I identify the source material?
- Did I set limits and exclusions?
- Did I require safeguards?
- Did I specify the tone?
- Did I control the order of work?
- Did I require a final stress test?
