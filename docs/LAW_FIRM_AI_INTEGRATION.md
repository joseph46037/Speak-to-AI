# Law Firm AI Integration With Speak to AI

This guide adapts the 11S Method for law-firm AI adoption.

The goal is not to replace lawyers. The goal is to create supervised, source-controlled, confidentiality-conscious workflows that reduce wasted time and improve work product.

AI can help a law firm draft, summarize, classify, compare, organize, and quality-check work. It cannot exercise independent legal judgment, verify its own legal authority, protect privilege by itself, or replace attorney responsibility.

---

## Core Law-Firm Rule

Use AI as a supervised legal-production assistant.

Do not treat AI output as filing-ready, client-ready, or authority-ready until an attorney verifies the facts, law, source material, citations, procedural posture, deadlines, and final judgment.

---

## Safe First Uses

Start with lower-risk internal work:

1. Intake summaries.
2. Case chronologies.
3. Evidence maps.
4. Drafting shells.
5. Discovery organization.
6. Client-status drafts.
7. Administrative SOPs.
8. Marketing drafts that do not reveal client information.
9. Billing narratives reviewed by a human.
10. Training materials.

Do not begin with unsupervised legal research or court filings.

---

## Law-Firm Risk Gate

Do not enter the following into an unapproved AI tool:

- privileged communications,
- confidential client facts,
- medical records,
- financial records,
- tax records,
- criminal-defense facts,
- family-law facts involving children,
- Social Security numbers,
- settlement communications,
- sealed filings,
- protective-order materials,
- discovery subject to confidentiality orders,
- immigration records,
- or any other sensitive material without attorney approval and an approved tool/workflow.

## Five Law-Firm Power Prompt Topics

A strong law-firm prompt library should include at least these five prompts:

1. Client intake analysis.
2. Chronology and evidence map.
3. Discovery request and objection drafting.
4. Motion or brief outline with authority checklist.
5. Client communication and case-status explanation.

Each prompt should include:

- role,
- task,
- jurisdiction,
- practice area,
- procedural posture,
- known facts,
- disputed facts,
- source materials,
- confidentiality limits,
- output format,
- attorney-review warning,
- citation-verification requirement,
- error-check instruction,
- missing-information list,
- and final quality-control checklist.

---

## Law-Firm Stress Test

Before relying on any AI-assisted law-firm output, check:

1. Is the jurisdiction correct?
2. Is the procedural posture correct?
3. Are the facts supported by source material?
4. Are citations verified?
5. Are local rules checked?
6. Is adverse authority identified?
7. Are assumptions separated from facts?
8. Is privileged or confidential information protected?
9. Does the output avoid legal advice by nonlawyer staff?
10. Has an attorney reviewed the final version?

A law firm does not need a faster hallucination machine. It needs a supervised production system that saves time without creating a disciplinary exhibit.
